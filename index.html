                        Why a beer graph, in a browser?

                        I make no excuses: I am a salesperson, and for about ten years I sold graphs at Neo4j. And one of my favourite ways to explain what a graph database actually does was always... beer. Belgian beer, to be precise.

                        Back in 2013 I took the Wikipedia list of Belgian beers and loaded it into a very simple graph: beers, breweries, beer types and alcohol percentages. That's it. And yet - it turned out to be a wonderful way to show people how connected data lets you spot patterns that you would never see in a table. I have shown that demo to so many people over the years, and even told the story on stage at Monki Gras (that's the video on this page).

                        The BeerGraph kept coming back, too: in Neo4j Bloom, with the security features of Neo4j 4.0, and in yet another way of importing that Wikipedia page. All of those articles are listed below.

                        So when Neo4j launched relate - an embedded graph database for agents that even runs inside your browser as WebAssembly - I really could not resist. How hard could it be to put the whole BeerGraph in a browser tab? This page is the result: no server, no install, just relate running in a Web Worker and keeping its data in your browser's own storage. On top of the classic graph recommendations I added vector similarity, a path finder and a little sommelier agent that uses the graph as its memory. I built it together with Claude Code for the Relate by Neo4j Hackathon, and it was tons of fun.

                        Hope you enjoy it as much as I did - and please, drink responsibly!
